Haven Health Management.

Designing clearer, more consistent digital experiences across a behavioral healthcare organization.

As a full-time UX/UI Designer on Haven's internal team, I translated complex treatment, location, insurance, and educational content into clear responsive experiences and reusable UI patterns.

Overview

A lot of information, for people who need it to be clear.

Haven Health Management is a behavioral healthcare organization with treatment centers across the U.S. Its websites explain treatment options, programs, locations, insurance, and educational resources, while keeping calling, contacting admissions, and checking insurance easy to find.

The challenge wasn't designing individual pages. It was keeping a large amount of healthcare information clear and consistent everywhere it appeared.

I translated these requirements into page structures, responsive layouts, and reusable UI patterns, including a full redesign of the Recovering Champions website, and worked with developers through handoff.

01 / Information hierarchy

Making complex healthcare content easier to navigate.

Problem

Treatment pages carry levels of care, therapies, medication, support groups, and aftercare. Shown all at once, it reads like a wall of clinical detail to someone who is already overwhelmed.

02 / Paths to care

Creating clear paths to care.

Problem

People arrive at very different moments. Some are researching for a family member. Some need help today. One "Contact us" button serves neither.

Decision

Every page offers a next step at three levels of readiness, so nobody has to jump from reading straight to a phone call.

1
2

Admissions

Make reaching out feel safe.

  1. 1
    What happens after you reach outAssessment, insurance review, and arrival sit beside the form.
  2. 2
    Call or check coverageTwo equal choices, so the softer step is never hidden.

Consistency

Shared patterns, connected experiences.

I created and reused patterns for recurring content: insurance, reviews, FAQs, and contact sections. Different pages feel like one experience, and developers could implement each pattern consistently instead of rebuilding it page by page.

Outcome
